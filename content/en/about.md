This blog began with a simple wish: not to let the final answer erase the path that led to it.

I study mathematics and especially enjoy problems that resist. The kind where an attempt looks promising, a calculation reveals a contradiction, an idea has to be abandoned — and, little by little, the problem begins to show what it was really asking.

That is the process I want to record here.

## What you will find

Mostly writing about mathematics, olympiad problems, proofs, and ideas that emerge while studying. But I do not want the blog to be limited to solutions. Books, reading, language, science, and other curiosities may also find their way into the margins.

This is not a course, a collection of ready-made answers, or a preparation manual. It is a public notebook: a post may begin with a question, follow an attempt, and end with a conclusion — or with a better question.

## How I write

I prefer to explain why an idea appeared, what it solved, and where it stopped working. When a proof is ready, I want it to be readable; when the path toward it is interesting, I do not want to hide the detours.

Mathematics requires precision. An account of learning also requires honesty. Neither should be an excuse for the other.

The name of this place is a reminder: sometimes, what seems to belong in the margin turns out to be the most interesting part of the page.

Posts may appear in Portuguese or English. Both versions aim to preserve the same idea, not merely replace one word with another.
